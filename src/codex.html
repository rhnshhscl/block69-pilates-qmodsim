Block 69 Pilates: proposal-to-module mapping

Block 69 Cafe (444 Kamagong St., Comembo, Taguig City) runs Pilates from Thursday to Saturday, with two fixed sessions a day: 9:00–9:50 AM and 10:15–11:05 AM. Each session has an assigned capacity of three or four clients, and admins cannot set it above four. The fee is ₱500, with an optional ₱50 mat rental.

In the proposed system:

  • Clients register, disclosing any injuries or medical conditions with consent. They view open sessions, reserve, and pay either through an online payment gateway or by uploading proof of a GCash payment.
  • Administrators open sessions in advance and cancel sessions that won't push through. They approve, reject, reschedule or cancel reservations, verify GCash proofs, and mark attendance.
  • Payment status (unpaid, pending verification, paid) and booking status (pending, confirmed, rescheduled, rejected, cancelled) are recorded separately. A booking is confirmed only after admin approval.
  • Clients get email notifications when a booking is confirmed, rescheduled or cancelled. Refunds are handled manually under the café's policy.

This simulator stress-tests that design. Each stressor maps to one QMODSIM module, and every number comes from the pure functions in src/engines/qmodsim.js.

Mod 1 · 3 · Stochastic processes & arrival rates

Non-homogeneous Poisson flash crowd

Time t is measured in minutes from the moment the admin opens the week's six sessions (an "Open Session" in the proposal). Background refreshes arrive at λ_base, and the release adds a Gaussian surge of height A centred at t_peak with spread σ.

λ(t) = λ_base + A · exp(−(t − t_peak)² / (2σ²)) Λ(T₀, T₁) = ∫ λ(t) dt = λ_base · (T₁ − T₀) + A · σ · √(π/2) · [erf((T₁ − t_peak)/(σ√2)) − erf((T₀ − t_peak)/(σ√2))] P(N(T₀,T₁) = k) = Λᵏ · e^(−Λ) / k! f(t) = λ · e^(−λt) (inter-arrival density at a constant rate λ)
  1. Substitute u = (t − t_peak)/(σ√2), so dt = σ√2 du. The Gaussian term then integrates to A · σ√2 · (√π / 2) · [erf(u)], which equals A · σ · √(π/2) · [erf(u)].
  2. Arrivals are generated by Lewis–Shedler thinning: candidates come from a homogeneous process at λ_max = λ_base + A, and each is accepted with probability λ(t)/λ_max.
  3. When t_peak sits well inside the window, about 68.3% of the surge falls within t_peak ± σ, since erf(1/√2) ≈ 0.683.

Mod 6 · Queueing theory

M/M/c/K slot-hold queue

A booking attempt that reaches checkout holds one client slot while the client pays through the gateway. c = 4 holds run in parallel, matching the maximum assigned capacity of four clients. Hold times are exponential with mean 1/μ, and at most K checkout sessions can hold or wait. Attempt number K + 1 is turned away with "Slots busy, try again".

a = λ / μ ρ = λ / (c · μ) Pₙ = P₀ · aⁿ / n! for 0 ≤ n < c Pₙ = P₀ · aⁿ / (c! · cⁿ⁻ᶜ) for c ≤ n ≤ K P₀ = [ Σₙ₌₀ᶜ⁻¹ aⁿ/n! + Σₙ₌ᶜᴷ aⁿ/(c! · cⁿ⁻ᶜ) ]⁻¹ P_drop = P_K = P₀ · cᶜ · ρᴷ / c! L = Σ n · Pₙ L_q = Σₙ₌ᶜ₊₁ᴷ (n − c) · Pₙ λ_eff = λ · (1 − P_K) W_q = L_q / λ_eff W = W_q + 1/μ Little's Law: L = λ_eff · W
  1. Birth–death balance gives Pₙ₊₁ · min(n + 1, c) · μ = Pₙ · λ. Chaining these yields the product form above.
  2. The engine works with log-terms ln Pₙ and subtracts the maximum before exponentiating, so K up to 200 and ρ ≫ 1 never overflow.
  3. W is built from L_q and 1/μ, not from L. The check L = λ_eff · W then holds only if the expected number of busy holds equals λ_eff/μ (flow conservation), so it is a real test.
  4. For the time-varying λ(t) in Scenario 1, P_drop(t) uses the pointwise stationary approximation: the steady-state formula is evaluated at each λ(t). Turned-away attempts = ∫ λ(t) · P_K(t) dt.

Mod 5 · Markov chains

6-state absorbing reservation chain

The states follow the proposal's lifecycle:

  • S0 Selecting: the client is browsing the schedule.
  • S1 Slot Held: payment status is unpaid, or pending verification for a GCash proof.
  • S2 Paid: payment status is paid and the booking is pending, confirmed or rescheduled while it waits for the session. A reschedule keeps the reservation in S2.
  • S3 Attended (absorbing): the admin marks the client present.
  • S4 Expired / Rejected / Cancelled (absorbing): covers hold expiry, admin rejection, client or admin cancellation, and a session that does not push through. Refunds happen outside the system.
  • S5 No-Show (absorbing): the admin marks the client absent.

Each transient row's self-loop is 1 minus its exits. If the exits add up to more than 1, they are rescaled to sum to 1.

P = [ Q R ] Q: 3 × 3 transient → transient [ 0 I ] R: 3 × 3 transient → absorbing N = (I − Q)⁻¹ expected visits to each transient state B = N · R absorption probabilities (rows sum to 1) t = N · 1 expected steps before absorption Conversion(attended) = B[S0, S3] Conversion(expired / rejected / cancelled) = B[S0, S4]
  1. N = I + Q + Q² + … converges because every transient state reaches an absorbing state, so Qᵏ → 0.
  2. The engine inverts I − Q by Gauss–Jordan elimination with partial pivoting, and the V&V drawer checks N · (I − Q) = I.
  3. LAB compares B[S0, ·] against a token simulation of the same chain.

Mod 7 · Game theory in network defence

Anti-scalping normal-form game

With only 3–4 slots per session, popular weekend slots can be worth reselling. The defender (studio) chooses D1 Rate Limit + CAPTCHA or D2 Deposit + SMS OTP. The attacker (reseller) chooses A1 Sniping or A2 Lock & Abandon DoS. All payoffs are in pesos per weekly release.

U_A(D1,A1) = (1 − r)·G − c_cap U_D(D1,A1) = −(1 − r)·g − f₁ U_A(D1,A2) = (1 − 0.4r)·H − c_cap U_D(D1,A2) = −(1 − 0.4r)·p_slot − f₁ U_A(D2,A1) = G − c_otp U_D(D2,A1) = −g − f₂ U_A(D2,A2) = H − F − c_otp U_D(D2,A2) = 0.5·F − f₂ p* = (U_A(D2,A2) − U_A(D2,A1)) / (U_A(D1,A1) − U_A(D2,A1) − U_A(D1,A2) + U_A(D2,A2)) q* = (U_D(D2,A2) − U_D(D1,A2)) / (U_D(D1,A1) − U_D(D1,A2) − U_D(D2,A1) + U_D(D2,A2))
  1. p* is the probability of D1 that makes the attacker indifferent between A1 and A2. q* is the probability of A1 that makes the defender indifferent.
  2. At the baseline (p_slot = ₱500), CAPTCHA pushes attackers to DoS, a deposit pushes them back to sniping, and against sniping the studio prefers rate limiting. The best responses cycle, so the only equilibrium is mixed: p* ≈ 0.577, q* ≈ 0.651.
  3. The guide's inspection game is a special case. Set attacker gain G, fine F, audit cost C_audit, and let undetected cheating cost the defender G + F. The general solver then returns P(cheat) = C_audit/(G + F) and P(audit) = G/(G + F), which are the guide's p* and q*. A unit test checks this reduction.
  4. LAB also plays fictitious play: each side best-responds to the other's empirical frequency. In 2 × 2 games the path converges to the equilibrium.
Mod 2 · 10 · Security modelling & information entropy

Booking-token entropy and keyspace

The confirmation, reschedule and cancellation emails carry a booking reference that opens the reservation. If the reference is short, anyone can enumerate it and view another client's booking.

H(X) = −Σ P(xᵢ) · log₂ P(xᵢ) = log₂(|Σ|ᴸ) = L · log₂|Σ| (uniform symbols) S = |Σ|ᴸ T_crack = S / (2 · R) 4-digit numeric: H = 4 · log₂ 10 ≈ 13.29 bits, S = 10⁴ 12-char alphanumeric: H = 12 · log₂ 62 ≈ 71.45 bits, S ≈ 3.2 × 10²¹
  1. At R = 10 guesses/s, a 4-digit code falls in about 500 s on average (8.3 minutes). A 12-char token needs about 5 × 10¹² years.
  2. Tokens come from crypto.getRandomValues(), never Math.random(). Bytes ≥ 248 are rejected so each of the 62 symbols has probability exactly 1/62.
  3. LAB measures the entropy per character of 1,500 generated tokens and compares it with log₂ 62 ≈ 5.954 bits.
Mod 12 · System reliability & availability

Series booking pipeline with redundant webhooks

A booking is confirmed only after admin approval, and approval needs a recorded payment. The proposal gives two independent ways to record one, so payment recording is a parallel group of two series routes. The gateway route has its own parallel group of status paths.

Rᵢ(t) = e^(−t / MTBFᵢ) R_series(t) = Π Rᵢ(t) R_parallel(t) = 1 − Π (1 − Rᵢ(t)) Aᵢ = MTBFᵢ / (MTBFᵢ + MTTRᵢ) A_sys composed the same way Pipeline: Edge/CDN → Web app & API → Slot-hold service → Database → { [Payment gateway → (Webhook ∥ Polling ∥ Retry queue)] ∥ [Proof upload & storage → Admin verification] } → Email notifications R_payment = 1 − (1 − R_gateway · R_status) · (1 − R_upload · R_verify)

Mod 4 · Monte Carlo & FAIR

Annual loss from release abuse

TEF ~ Beta-PERT(a_min, m_likely, b_max), α = 1 + 4(m − a)/(b − a), β = 1 + 4(b − m)/(b − a) N | TEF ~ Poisson(TEF · V) LM_primary ~ Lognormal(median, σ), LM_secondary ~ Lognormal(median₂, σ₂) with probability P_sec Loss_Total = Σ (Loss_Primary + Loss_Secondary) ALE = E[N] · E[S], E[S] = median·e^(σ²/2) + P_sec · median₂·e^(σ₂²/2) ROSI = [(ALE_base − ALE_mitigated) − C_control] / C_control
  1. Primary loss per event covers manual refunds and empty-slot revenue at ₱500 per slot across up to 24 slots per week (6 sessions × 4). Secondary loss is the RA 10173 response cost when client data is exposed. It is weighted higher because registration collects health disclosures.
  2. The histogram and P90/P99 use the raw trial losses. The ALE estimate uses conditional Monte Carlo (Rao–Blackwell): it averages E[Loss | N] = N · E[S], which is unbiased and cuts the seed-to-seed spread caused by rare, large secondary losses.
  3. The Deposit + OTP control scales V by (1 − e). The cost of the control enters through ROSI.
Statutory compliance

RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012) crosswalk

The system stores client names, email addresses, booking, payment and attendance records, GCash payment screenshots, and health disclosures (injuries, medical conditions, physical limitations). The proposal already commits to collecting health data with consent, showing it only to administrators, and handling it under RA 10173. Health information counts as sensitive personal information, so it carries stricter rules than the rest. Have your adviser confirm the section mapping before the defense.
